## Diagnosis

Three findings, in priority order:

1. **SQL correctness, not SQL syntax.** All three SQL questions share a property:
   the wrong answer produces no error. An empty result set, an inflated total, a
   silent duplicate. This is the data science → data engineering gap showing up
   directly — I've been writing SQL to find answers I'd then eyeball, not SQL
   that has to be right for people who will never check it.

2. **Grain and idempotency both missed.** These are the two load-bearing concepts
   on the sheet. Grain is the claim about what one row represents; idempotency is
   the guarantee that a rerun changes nothing. Most of the rest of data
   engineering hangs off these two.

3. **Four correct answers I couldn't defend.** Distinct failure mode from being
   wrong: in an interview, a follow-up question talks me out of a right answer.
   The fix is producing the *because* in writing, which is what `concepts/` is for.

**Deferred deliberately:** Spark internals and streaming semantics. Both were
single questions — one question is an anecdote, not a measurement — and both sit
below SQL, modeling and pipeline hygiene in what junior roles actually screen for.

## What the calibration says

Zero blind spots across fifteen questions. I never marked *certain* and got it
wrong, and the six I marked as guesses were exactly the six I missed. Worth more
than a few extra points would have been: confidently-wrong engineers ship
confidently-wrong data, and my self-assessment can be trusted to direct study.
